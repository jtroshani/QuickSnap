How it works

Three keys and a drag

STEP 01

Press ⌘⇧2

From any app. Your pointer turns into a crosshair.

STEP 02

Drag over what you want

A whole window? Press Space first, then click it.

STEP 03

Hit Copy

The markup bar — highlighter, arrow, box, text — opens first. Or skip it. Then ⌘V anywhere.

Prefer a file? Save drops one PNG on your Desktop and shows it in Finder. Nothing touches your disk unless you ask it to.

Why it exists

Small on purpose

macOS already has ⌘⇧⌃4. QuickSnap is that same system selector with a shortcut you'll actually remember, a menu-bar icon you can find, and a markup step built in.

No Electron. No account. No telemetry. It's a native Swift app about the size of a photo, and the source is right there on GitHub.

Opens at login if you want it to. Quits from the same little menu. That's the whole surface area — a six-year-old gets it on the first try.

First launch

  1. Unzip and drag QuickSnap into your Applications folder.
  2. Right-click it → Open → Open. One time only — it isn't signed with a paid Apple certificate, so the first open needs your OK.
  3. Allow Screen Recording when macOS asks. Every screenshot tool needs this. Turn QuickSnap on, then reopen it — the permission only applies on relaunch.
